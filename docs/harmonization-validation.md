## 5. Terra-only era (2000-11 – 2002-06)

Terra → Terra∪Aqua scaling validated by leave-one-year-out over 2002-07 – 2021 (234 months): MAE 408, RMSE 589, mean signed error −44 (−0.5 %), r = 0.994. Regional factors: day 2.24, night 1.35. v2 held-out interval for Terra-only months: ×[0.912, 1.119], out-of-sample coverage 88.5 %.

## 7. Coefficients (region level)

MODIS / VIIRS-reference per overpass: day 0.587, night 0.243. The day ratio is stable through the year (0.53–0.68); the night ratio is strongly seasonal (0.047 in January to 0.350 in July): VIIRS detects far more small or cool night fires, especially outside the main fire season. Day+night overlap factor ρ: 0.997 (winter) to 0.897 (August). 866 zone coefficients are published in `public/data/v2/us/calibration.json`.

## 8. Orbit-drift diagnostic (why 2022+ is VIIRS-estimated)

| Year | Observed MODIS | VIIRS-estimated MODIS-equivalent | Observed / estimated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 108,208 | 113,080 | 0.957 |
| 2023 | 82,427 | 86,478 | 0.953 |
| 2024 | 107,066 | 104,524 | 1.024 |
| 2025 | 93,403 | 103,420 | 0.903 |
| 2026 (to Nov 14) | 91,838 | 100,609 | 0.913 |

In 2012–2021 the same ratio scatters around 1 (range 0.950–1.071, mean 1.000). From 2025 observed MODIS falls ~9–10 % below the stable-orbit expectation, consistent with Terra's earlier and Aqua's later crossing times sampling a different part of the diurnal fire cycle. This supports using VIIRS-based estimates from 2022; it is not independent proof, because fire-regime change can also move the ratio.

## Limits

- Validation compares against MODIS itself (the target scale), not against ground truth. It shows that MODIS-equivalent values are reproducible from VIIRS, not that either sensor measures true fire.
- All held-out years come from one decade (2012–2021). A second region (Iberia, below) shows the US method choice does not transfer automatically.
- Interval coverage is slightly under nominal (US 89.1 %, Iberia 88.2 %).
- The 2022 switch date is a documented choice; the drift table lets users judge it. Raw MODIS for 2022+ stays available.
- No cloud/smoke or observation-opportunity correction; zero means no matching records.

### What Iberia shows

1. **The pipeline transfers unchanged** (config only: box, clip polygon, sources). Download, clip, static mask, aggregation, publishing, verification and fitting ran with no code changes.
2. **The adopted method does not win everywhere.** In a small region (≈ 4 % of US fire-cell-days) the zone × month × overpass ratios overfit; quantile mapping has 27 % lower MAE and less than half the RMSE.
3. **Linear ratios under-estimate extreme fire years.** The two largest held-out years (2012, 2017 — the 2017 Portugal/Galicia fires) come out 18–20 % low, and 2022 (observed 25 % above the estimate) is the same signal. Large, intense fire seasons are detected relatively better by MODIS than ratio scaling from VIIRS assumes. In the US the effect is small (2017 0.950); in Iberia it decides rankings of the worst years. Quantile mapping, which is non-linear, is the natural fix.
4. **Consequence, applied as `fcd-harmonize-v2`:** the estimator is chosen per region by the pre-registered held-out MAE rule. Iberia now uses quantile mapping (AOIs: region estimate × AOI share of the VIIRS reference); the US keeps the stratified ratio.

The extreme years are fixed (2017: 0.821 → 1.012). In the US the same AOI-share rule would be much worse than zone ratios (median zone error 14.4 % vs 4.8 %), which is why the selection is per region, not global. Iberia's held-out years remain only ten, and the 2014 (+18 %) and 2021 (−11 %) years show small-sample error that the interval reflects.

Not measured: applying US coefficients to Iberia directly (cross-region transfer of the *coefficients*). Every region is fitted on its own 2012–2021 overlap, which any new region also has.
